## Root cause

PRV-27's two unstable captures each had a cause of their own. Both are reproduced, at the base capture code (2473f21, run from `git archive`) and at the head, by "real-board regeneration does not depend on host speed" (`e2e/fidelity-harness.spec.ts`). Each case runs one capture of a real board under a normal and a reproduced condition. At the base the two conditions give different values; at the head they give the same.

### `home/holly` transition start direction

- **What the harness did.** A holly state is one to five clicks on "Next photo of Holly" (`holly-4` is three). `runSteps` in `scripts/boards/capture.ts` ran a state's clicks back to back. Each click moves every photo in the deck to its next depth with `transition: transform 220ms ease`. When a click lands while the last click's slides are still moving, CSS starts each new transition from the photo's current, mid-flight transform. The recorder reads a transition's direction from that start value (`direction()` in `scripts/boards/inpage.js`), so `enter x+y-`, `enter x+y+` or `enter x-y+` depended on how far the last slide had got. That depends on how long the driver took between clicks: on a fast host the next click caught the slide early, on a loaded one late or not at all. Reference images did not move, because the deck settles to the same final state either way.
- **Deterministic reproduction.** Case `holly-start-direction` captures `light/holly-4` on `range-light-desktop.dc.html`. Normal: the page's animation clock runs at 1/100 speed (CDP `Animation.setPlaybackRate` 0.01), so every click lands before the last slide has moved, as on a host that is never behind. Reproduced: every click waits 600 ms first, longer than the 220 ms slide and the 300 ms tuck, as on a slow driver. At the base the `home/holly` motion records differ between the two; at the head they are identical.
- **Ruled out.** The photos, the clock and the board's own scripts: the deck's state after the clicks is the same in every run, and only the recorded start direction moves.
- **Same cause, other rows.** Every state of two or more steps ran the same way. The photo galleries' `gallery-3` to `gallery-6` (`seth/photos`, `christine/photos`, `post-life/gallery`) are two to five clicks on "Next photo", each a 200 ms `transition: opacity` fade. In the committed spec a click lands partway through the last fade, so a fade is recorded reversed, with a shortened duration (between 43.9 and 115.2 ms in place of 200 ms), and the fades that follow are recorded on different photos. PRV-27's run happened to match those values, but they are one sample of the same race. Case `gallery-click-mid-fade` captures `light/gallery-3` on `range-profile-seth-desktop.dc.html` under the two holly conditions. `chips-back`, the only other state of two steps, recorded the same motion before and after the fix.
- **Changed captures.** The fix changes the recorded motion of these rows in these states (the `-rm` and `+pre-…` captures of each state included), and nothing else: `home/holly`: `holly-3`, `holly-4`, `holly-5`, `holly-6`. `seth/photos`: `gallery-3`, `gallery-4`. `christine/photos`: `gallery-3`, `gallery-4`. `post-life/gallery`: `gallery-3`, `gallery-4`, `gallery-5`, `gallery-6`.

**Fix.** `captureState` now runs a state's steps with `runStepsAtRest`. After each step but the last, it waits for the step's motions to start (three frames and 60 ms, as `takeMotion` does), waits for a view transition to be ready, notes every running animation (`motionSweep()`, so one that ends early is still recorded), and settles the page. Every click then starts from rest, so each slide is recorded from the photo's resting transform. An in-flight state (the theme reveal) still runs its steps as before, because its hold is armed for the transition the steps start.

Regenerating all 25 boards with the fix changed only the recorded motion of the holly and gallery states of two or more steps (`holly-3` to `holly-6`, `gallery-3` to `gallery-6`, the `-rm` variants included), on the rows that have them. No reference file and no other value changed.

### `home/opener` tab-sites shot

- **What the harness did.** The opener's "Sites" tab swaps in the word "websites", which runs `word-in` (280 ms, `animation-fill-mode: both`) on an inline-block span. `settle()` finishes the animation, and because it fills, it still applies its end value as an effect. Chromium can then draw the word in two ways that differ in one glyph: the "t" of "websites" lands about 0.7 px apart. Layout is identical in both (every character's box is the same, and the word's left edge is at x = 461.89 px), and the probes' layer tree shows the word on a compositor layer of its own. `shoot()` takes full-page shots, and the first full-page shot of a page fixes which rendering every later shot of it shows. Straight after the settle it got either one; once the page had drawn frames for a while (150 ms or more, or a frame loop) it got the late one. At the base the shot came straight after the settle, so the rendering it got depended on host speed. Regeneration files identical shots of a board once, so `dark/tab-sites` and `dark/tab-sites+pre-D11+D13+D14`, which share one reference when both shots get the early rendering, were filed separately when one got the late one.
- **Deterministic reproduction.** Case `opener-tab-sites-shot` captures `dark/tab-sites` and `dark/tab-sites+pre-D11+D13+D14` on `range-dark-desktop.dc.html`, shoots `home/opener` in each, and counts the reference files the two shots would be filed under. Normal: the driver reads one frame of the viewport the moment the capture ends, and the shot follows straight after. A full-page shot that comes straight after a viewport read gets the early rendering, as one that lands straight after the settle does on a fast host. Reproduced: the page keeps drawing frames (a `requestAnimationFrame` loop), the driver stalls 800 ms between any read of the viewport coming back and its next request for a full-page shot (`stallAfterViewportRead()`), and the shot waits 800 ms, as on a loaded host. In the investigation probes at the base, the normal condition gave the early rendering in 8 of 8 runs and the reproduced one gave the late rendering in 12 of 12.
- **Ruled out.** Animation progress at the settle, the page's animation clock (CDP `Animation.setPlaybackRate`), main-thread CPU throttling (CDP `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate` at 20 and 200), `--disable-threaded-animation`, `--run-all-compositor-stages-before-draw`, the page's animation events (blocked), freezing the page, a style recalculation, a resize and a new `will-change` layer each left the shot to chance. Waiting is not a fix either: with frames drawn for 500 ms (and at least 30 frames) after the settle, the late rendering was still missed in 1 of 6 runs, and with Chromium's frame rate unthrottled, 400 ms of frames missed it in 3 of 6. Releasing the animation (`commitStyles()` and `cancel()`) does fix the shot, but it also changes how other rows draw beside an animated element (the compositor no longer keeps its layer), so it moved 60 of the 99 references on `range-dark-desktop.dc.html`, most of them captures that were stable. That is outside this ticket's scope, so it was not taken.

**Fix.** Before `captureState` hands a settled page back, it takes the page's first full-page shot itself (`fixRendering()` in `scripts/boards/capture.ts`): a read of the viewport and a one-pixel full-page shot, sent to Chromium together as one driver step (two `Page.captureScreenshot` requests on one CDP session, the second with `captureBeyondViewport`). Straight after a viewport read a finished, filling animation is drawn the early way, the way reduced motion draws it, and that first full-page shot fixes it for every full-page shot after it, however late they come. Because both requests leave the driver before the read comes back, no stall of the driver can fall between them. In-flight states are shot from the held viewport frame and are not affected.

What the probes showed (4 runs each, the page drawing frames throughout):

- A viewport read alone is not enough: with the read inside the capture, a full-page shot 300 ms or 800 ms later got the late rendering.
- Two driver calls are not enough either (the first version of this fix, at 0302b35): a viewport read, an 800 ms stall, then the full-page shot got the late rendering, and so did the same sequence over raw CDP.
- A full-page shot on its own, without the viewport read, got the late rendering in 1 of 4 runs.
- The two requests sent together, 800 ms after the capture, got the early rendering in every run, and every shot 800 ms later matched it. So did the same with Chromium's frame rate unthrottled.

At 0302b35 the case's reproduced condition shot both captures with the late rendering, where the normal condition shot the committed early one; at the head both conditions give the committed shot, filed once. Regenerating every board with the fix, normally and under the slow host, filed `dark/tab-sites` and `dark/tab-sites+pre-D11+D13+D14` under the one committed reference, as before, and left every other reference and spec value as committed.

## Known gaps

- The opener fix takes the two shots in one driver step, so a stall of the driver cannot fall between them. Chromium still serves the two requests one after the other, so a stall inside the browser itself of about 150 ms of drawn frames, the time the late rendering took to land in the probes, between serving the first and starting the second could still get the late rendering. No probe condition did, with the frame rate unthrottled included. Controlling Chromium's frames outright (`--enable-begin-frame-control`) would close it, but Playwright's screenshots do not run under it.
- The investigation probes (`requestAnimationFrame` loop, CDP flags and throttling, layer-tree reads) were scratch scripts and are not committed; the committed cases reproduce the two conditions that matter.
- The AC2 cases slow the host deterministically (an animation clock at 1/100 speed, a 600 ms wait before each click, a frame loop, an 800 ms stall after each read of the viewport and an 800 ms wait before the shot) rather than loading the machine, as the ticket allows. The AC3 "slow host" run applies the same waits to `npm run boards:regen` through a Node preload (`SLOW_HOST` in `e2e/fidelity-harness.spec.ts`), and regenerates every board, one per run, into one output tree per condition before comparing its whole `spec.json` and `ref/` with the committed ones.

- The ticket audit stopped after 2 rounds, per the portfolio ticket-audit cap (owner, 2026-11-24). Round 2's three warnings (AC2 base-and-head comparison, manifest unchanged, CI result recorded rather than mapped to a scenario) are applied in this final fix pass and not re-audited.
